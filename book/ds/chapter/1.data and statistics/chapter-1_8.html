Skewness & Kurtosis

18 min read Intermediate Distribution Analysis

Skewness and kurtosis describe the shape of data distributions. While mean and standard deviation tell us about center and spread, these measures reveal asymmetry and tail behavior—critical for understanding data characteristics.

What is Skewness?

Skewness is a measure of the asymmetry or skew of a probability distribution. It tells us whether data is distributed symmetrically around the mean or if it leans more to one side.

Key Insight: Skewness quantifies how much a distribution deviates from being symmetric. A perfectly symmetric distribution (like the normal distribution) has zero skewness.

Skewness Formula

The formula for skewness (using the third moment about the mean):

$$\gamma_1 = \frac{n}{(n-1)(n-2)} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left(\frac{X_i - \bar{X}}{s}\right)^3$$

Where:

$\gamma_1$ (Gamma)

The skewness coefficient

$n$

Number of data points

$X_i$

Individual data points

$\bar{X}$

Sample mean

$s$

Sample standard deviation

Types of Skewness

Negative Skew (Left-Skewed)

Skewness < 0

  • Long tail extends to the left
  • Mean < Median < Mode
  • Majority of data on the right
  • Example: Age at retirement (max ~70)

Zero Skew (Symmetric)

Skewness ≈ 0

  • Perfectly symmetric distribution
  • Mean = Median = Mode
  • Equal tails on both sides
  • Example: Normal distribution

Positive Skew (Right-Skewed)

Skewness > 0

  • Long tail extends to the right
  • Mode < Median < Mean
  • Majority of data on the left
  • Example: Income distribution

Worked Example: Computing Skewness

Let's calculate skewness for the dataset: [1, 2, 5, 7, 10]

1

Calculate Mean and Standard Deviation

Mean: $\bar{X} = \frac{1+2+5+7+10}{5} = 5$

Sample Std Dev: $s \approx 3.67$

2

Calculate Standardized Deviations (Cubed)

For $X_i = 1$: $\left(\frac{1-5}{3.67}\right)^3 = (-1.09)^3 \approx -1.30$

For $X_i = 2$: $\left(\frac{2-5}{3.67}\right)^3 = (-0.82)^3 \approx -0.55$

For $X_i = 5$: $\left(\frac{5-5}{3.67}\right)^3 = (0)^3 = 0$

For $X_i = 7$: $\left(\frac{7-5}{3.67}\right)^3 = (0.55)^3 \approx 0.16$

For $X_i = 10$: $\left(\frac{10-5}{3.67}\right)^3 = (1.36)^3 \approx 2.52$

3

Apply the Skewness Formula

Sum of cubed deviations: $-1.30 - 0.55 + 0 + 0.16 + 2.52 = 0.83$

$$\gamma_1 = \frac{5}{(5-1)(5-2)} \times 0.83 = \frac{5}{12} \times 0.83 \approx 0.35$$

Result: Skewness ≈ 0.35 (slightly positive/right-skewed)

Interpretation

A skewness of 0.35 indicates a slight positive skew. The distribution has a longer tail on the right side, meaning there are some higher values pulling the mean upward.

What is Kurtosis?

Kurtosis is a statistical measure that quantifies the shape and peakedness (or flatness) of a probability distribution. It provides information about the tails of the distribution and the presence of outliers.

Remember: Kurtosis measures the "tailedness" of a distribution—how much probability mass is in the tails versus the center.

Kurtosis Formula

The formula for excess kurtosis (fourth moment about the mean):

$$\text{Kurtosis} = \frac{n(n+1)}{(n-1)(n-2)(n-3)} \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left(\frac{X_i - \bar{X}}{s}\right)^4 - \frac{3(n-1)^2}{(n-2)(n-3)}$$

Note: The subtraction of 3 gives us "excess kurtosis," which makes the normal distribution have a kurtosis of 0.

Types of Kurtosis

Leptokurtic (Kurtosis > 0)

  • Heavy tails, sharp peak
  • More outliers than normal
  • Higher probability in tails
  • Example: Financial returns during crisis

Mesokurtic (Kurtosis ≈ 0)

  • Normal distribution
  • Moderate peak and tails
  • Reference distribution (baseline)
  • Example: Heights, test scores

Platykurtic (Kurtosis < 0)

  • Light tails, flat peak
  • Fewer outliers than normal
  • Lower probability in tails
  • Example: Uniform distribution

"Kurtosis is not about the peak—it's about the tails. High kurtosis means you should expect more extreme values."

— Statistical Insight

Python Implementation

Here's how to calculate skewness and kurtosis in Python:

import pandas as pd
from scipy import stats

# Sample data
data = [1, 2, 5, 7, 10]

# Method 1: Using pandas
df = pd.Series(data)
skewness = df.skew()
kurtosis = df.kurt()  # Returns excess kurtosis

print(f"Skewness (pandas): {skewness:.3f}")
print(f"Kurtosis (pandas): {kurtosis:.3f}")

# Method 2: Using scipy
skewness_scipy = stats.skew(data)
kurtosis_scipy = stats.kurtosis(data)  # Excess kurtosis by default

print(f"Skewness (scipy): {skewness_scipy:.3f}")
print(f"Kurtosis (scipy): {kurtosis_scipy:.3f}")

# Output:
# Skewness (pandas): 0.353
# Kurtosis (pandas): -1.292

Interpretation Guidelines

Skewness Interpretation

Skewness Value Interpretation
-0.5 to 0.5 Fairly symmetric
-1 to -0.5 or 0.5 to 1 Moderately skewed
< -1 or > 1 Highly skewed

Kurtosis Interpretation

Excess Kurtosis Type Meaning
> 0 Leptokurtic Heavy tails, more outliers
≈ 0 Mesokurtic Normal-like distribution
< 0 Platykurtic Light tails, fewer outliers

Practical Applications

Finance & Risk

High kurtosis in stock returns indicates higher risk of extreme movements. Skewness helps assess asymmetric risk profiles.

Machine Learning

Understanding distribution shape helps choose appropriate algorithms and transformations (e.g., log transform for right-skewed data).

Quality Control

Detecting deviations from normality in manufacturing processes. Helps identify systemic issues or process shifts.

Research & Testing

Many statistical tests assume normality. Checking skewness and kurtosis validates these assumptions.

Key Takeaways

  • Skewness measures asymmetry: negative (left), zero (symmetric), positive (right)
  • Kurtosis measures tail heaviness: more outliers = higher kurtosis
  • Both are critical for understanding data distribution beyond mean and standard deviation
  • Use Python's pandas or scipy for easy calculation
  • Important for choosing appropriate statistical methods and transformations
  • Essential for risk assessment, quality control, and ML preprocessing
